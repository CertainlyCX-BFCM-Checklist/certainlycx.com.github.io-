The BFCM CX Checklist

Ten things to get right before your busiest week of the year — people, queues, promos, AI, and the after-hours plan that keeps a bad night from becoming a bad quarter.

01

People & Coverage

  • Look at last year's BFCM ticket volume and compare it with what you're expecting this year.
  • Figure out when your busiest hours usually are and check whether your current team can cover them.
  • Check your evenings and weekends. Who is handling customers when your normal team is offline?
  • Decide what you will do if ticket volume is 2x or 3x higher than normal.
  • Have a backup plan for sick days, call-outs, or people who can't make their shifts.
02

Queue & Workflow

  • Look at the types of tickets you usually get during BFCM and decide which ones need a fast response.
  • Check that agents know when to handle something themselves and when to escalate it.
  • Make sure two or three agents aren't working on the same customer issue.
  • Review your current SLAs and decide whether they still make sense for BFCM.
  • Decide what happens when the queue starts getting out of control.
03

Promos, Orders & Shipping

  • Check that support has the latest information on every BFCM promotion and discount.
  • Check what products may run out of stock and make sure support knows.
  • Check your shipping cutoffs and expected delivery times.
  • Make sure agents know what to tell customers when an order is delayed or stuck with a carrier.
  • Review your return, refund, exchange, and cancellation policies before the rush.
04

Knowledge & Macros

  • Look at the questions customers asked most during last year's BFCM.
  • Turn the most common questions into simple macros or saved replies.
  • Check that your help center has the latest information.
  • Make sure agents can find important answers without asking a manager every time.
  • Test your macros before the rush instead of finding out they're wrong when the queue is full.
05

After-Hours

  • Look at what happens to tickets after your normal team logs off.
  • Figure out who handles overnight tickets and who is covering weekends.
  • Decide which issues can wait until the next business day and which ones need someone immediately.
  • Make sure there is a clear handoff between shifts.
  • Check what happens to an urgent customer issue that comes in at 10 p.m. on Black Friday.
06

AI & Automation

  • Look at what questions your AI or automation is currently handling.
  • Check that order tracking, shipping, return, and other automated answers are accurate.
  • Test your automation using real BFCM-type questions.
  • Figure out which questions should always go to a person.
  • Make sure customers can reach a person when automation can't solve the problem.
07

Watch the Queue

  • Watch which types of tickets start increasing once BFCM begins.
  • Look for sudden spikes in order-status, shipping, return, refund, or product questions.
  • Check whether one product, promotion, or shipping issue is creating a large number of tickets.
  • Watch response times and backlog as volume changes.
  • If the same problem keeps coming up, figure out whether you can fix the problem instead of answering the same question over and over.
08

Take Care of the Team

  • Check whether agents have somewhere to raise problems they keep seeing.
  • Watch workload, not just ticket numbers.
  • Make sure managers have backup when things get busy.
  • Have a plan for what happens when someone calls out during the busiest period.
  • Check in with the team before the rush, not only when people are already overwhelmed.
09

When Something Goes Wrong

  • Figure out who owns major customer problems.
  • Decide who handles shipping, inventory, website, payment, and order issues.
  • Make sure agents know who to contact when they can't solve something.
  • Decide what agents can offer customers without waiting for approval.
  • Make sure major issues don't get lost between support and other teams.
10

After BFCM

  • Look at which questions created the most tickets.
  • Look at where customers waited the longest.
  • Look at which issues created the most work for agents.
  • Check what AI and automation handled well and where it struggled.
  • Write down the problems you don't want to repeat next year.
